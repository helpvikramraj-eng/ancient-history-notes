Chapter 4: Ancient Trade Routes

The economic history of the Indian subcontinent is deeply intertwined with its ancient commercial networks. During the early centuries, the region established profound connections with the Roman Empire, Southeast Asia, and Central Asian kingdoms. Silk, spices, textiles, and precious metals were frequently exchanged through well-documented land and maritime paths.

Historians argue that the stability of these trade routes depended largely on regional administrative policies. For instance, the reforms introduced during the Gupta period greatly incentivized local merchants. Security outposts were built at regular intervals along the national highways to protect caravans from local raiders.
